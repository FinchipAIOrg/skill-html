Core capabilities

Capabilities supported by current code

Based on repository v3.9.4, the investor database, and evaluator structure. Counts follow current code, not legacy document headings.

01 / MULTI-METHOD

66 methodology-based roles

Each role has its own field scope, rules, and references. Outputs simulate a methodology; they are not the person’s live opinions.

02 / STRUCTURED

Structured signals

Each role can output signal, score, confidence, verdict, and comment for comparison and report assembly.

03 / SCHOOLS

Nine investor schools

Classic value, growth, macro, technical, China value, A-share momentum, quant, technology leaders, and the AI bottleneck hunter.

04 / CONSENSUS

Consensus and signal distribution

Aggregate long-book signals into bullish / neutral / bearish distributions and a consensus score, while retaining short-risk observers separately.

05 / CROSS-MARKET

Cross-market input

The workflow targets A-share, Hong Kong, and U.S. equities. Actual coverage depends on source connectivity, ticker support, and optional API configuration.

06 / TRACEABLE

Cached, traceable output

The workflow can generate raw_data, dimensions, panel, synthesis, and HTML report artifacts.

How it works

From data to panel review

Data is collected and normalized into analytical dimensions, then evaluated through role-specific rules and scoring. Outputs preserve individual differences and generate an aggregate view.

01

Market & Company Data

Available market, financial, valuation, industry, event, flow, and sentiment data.

02

Dimensions

Normalize heterogeneous data into dimensions the evaluator can consume.

03

Methodologies

Each role reads only fields relevant to its methodology and applies its evaluation rules.

04

Signals

Generate individual signal, score, confidence, verdict, and reasoning.

05

Panel Review

Aggregate distribution, school-level disagreement, consensus, and the full research report.

Key boundary: panel roles are research simulations built on defined methodologies and rules. Labels such as “Buffett style” or “Soros style” refer to a methodological lens, not the actual person’s present opinion on a stock.

Reliability & limitations

Know what it can do—and what it cannot

A financial research tool earns trust not only through rich output, but through clear boundaries around data, methods, and roles.

Methodology boundary

A research-assistance and methodology-simulation system

Role names help users understand different investment frameworks. No role output should be described as a real person’s current opinion, recommendation, or trading instruction.

All three cases on this page are interface demonstrations. Their numbers, signals, confidence values, and comments are not derived from live markets and are not investment advice.
Data sourcesThe repository integrates multiple free data sources and optional APIs. Availability depends on regional networks, rate limits, and ticker coverage.
Free fallbackSome fields can fall back to free sources, but fallback does not guarantee completeness. Missing dimensions affect score quality.
Cache structureThe workflow can cache intermediate outputs such as raw_data.json, dimensions.json, panel.json, and synthesis.json.
Data freshnessFreshness depends on upstream update schedules and cache hits. Verify timestamps and source status before use.
Roles and rulesThe 66 roles follow field allowlists, rules, and prompts. They evaluate through a methodology; they are not digital replicas of people.
Count conventionCurrent code defines 66 roles across nine groups. Two short-mandate roles act as risk observers and are excluded from the long-book consensus distribution.
Output limitsConfidence is internal confidence in an evaluation, not the probability of profit. Review output against the underlying data.
